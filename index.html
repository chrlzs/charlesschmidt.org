About

I'm a software engineer by background and an engineering leader by profession. Over the years, I've worked as a developer, architect, consultant, and director across a variety of industries and technologies.

I enjoy solving technical problems, but I've learned that building good software involves more than choosing the right architecture or framework. It takes people who understand what they're trying to accomplish, have room to do their work, and can make decisions without fighting unnecessary complexity.

My approach to leadership is pretty straightforward: understand the problem, be clear about priorities, give people the tools and support they need, and stay close enough to the work to be useful.

I'm particularly interested in the systems that connect everything together—integrations, infrastructure, data pipelines, and the decisions that make software reliable and maintainable.

And despite spending much of my career in leadership roles, I haven't lost my interest in building things myself.

How I Work

Stay technical. I think engineering leaders make better decisions when they understand the systems and challenges their teams work with. That doesn't mean writing every line of code, but it does mean remaining curious and involved.

Keep things understandable. Complex systems are sometimes unavoidable. Unnecessary complexity isn't. Good architecture should help people understand, operate, and change the software they depend on.

Give people ownership. I prefer clear expectations, open communication, and trust over process for its own sake. Teams do their best work when they understand the goal and have the autonomy to reach it.

Keep learning. Technology doesn't stand still, and neither should we. I've spent my career learning new tools, revisiting old assumptions, and occasionally discovering that an approach I dismissed years ago wasn't such a bad idea after all.
